**Zenny Feature & Design Reference**

*For website, social, ads, and general design/copy use. Plain-language, customer-facing.*

## **1\. About Zenny**

Zenny is an AI customer agent built by **ZeroManual**. It lives on a business's website, socials, and messaging channels, and talks to customers the way a great employee would — answering questions, helping people decide, completing bookings/orders, and following up when someone doesn't finish.

Zenny isn't a generic FAQ bot bolted onto a website. It's built around real customer psychology: people trust businesses that are helpful and honest, not ones that pressure them. So Zenny is designed to guide, not push — no fake urgency, no manufactured scarcity, no pressure tactics. Just fast, competent, human-feeling help, every time, at any hour.

**Mission, in one line:** *make sure no customer ever gets ignored — and no business ever loses a sale to silence.*

## **2\. Features**

* **24/7 availability** — always on, no hold times, no "we'll get back to you"

* **Chat \+ Voice** — text conversation or spoken conversation (web call or phone)

* **Knows the business** — trained on the business's own info: products, services, policies, pricing, FAQs

* **Completes real actions** — books appointments, takes orders, creates reservations, registers sign-ups, directly inside the conversation

* **Remembers the conversation** — customers never have to repeat themselves

* **Follows up automatically** — reminds people who got distracted before finishing (cart, booking, inquiry)

* **Escalates when it should** — hands off to a real person for anything sensitive, high-value, or outside its depth

* **Multi-channel** — website, chat, social DMs, SMS, email, voice

* **Calendar-aware** — books directly into the business's existing calendar/scheduling system

* **Connects to business tools** — e.g. syncs with an online store to check real stock and recommend products, connects to booking/reservation systems, pulls order status from the business's existing platform

* **Dashboard & reporting** — the business owner can see everything Zenny is doing, in one place

* **Fits many business types** — service businesses, shops, restaurants, clinics, agencies, nonprofits, and more

## **3\. Common Integrations**

Zenny connects to the tools a business already uses, so it acts on real, live data — not guesses.

* **Online stores (e.g. Shopify-type platforms)** — Zenny sees real inventory and gives live product recommendations. If a shopper asks "do you have this in blue?", Zenny checks actual stock before answering — and won't recommend something that's sold out.

* **Booking/scheduling systems** — connects to the business's existing calendar so every booked slot is real and instantly confirmed, no double-booking.

* **Restaurant/reservation platforms** — checks real table availability and confirms reservations directly.

* **Payment/checkout systems** — guides customers to a secure checkout or completes the order directly, depending on setup.

* **CRM / customer records** — recognizes returning customers and keeps their history in view, so service feels continuous.

* **Email & SMS platforms** — sends confirmations, reminders, and follow-ups through the channels the business already uses.

* **Social & messaging apps** — WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook Messenger — same Zenny, same capability, wherever the customer messages from.

**Note for design/copy:** keep integration mentions generic-realistic ("connects to your online store," "syncs with your booking system") rather than naming specific software brands, unless a specific partnership is confirmed by the product team.

## **4\. How It Works**

A simple way to describe the flow (for a "How It Works" page, 3-5 steps):

**Step 1 — Customer reaches out.**

Through the website chat, a social DM, SMS, or a phone call — whichever channel they prefer.

**Step 2 — Zenny understands what they need.**

It reads the message (or listens, for voice) and figures out the intent: a question, a purchase, a booking, a complaint, a general inquiry.

**Step 3 — Zenny helps them get there.**

* If they already know what they want, Zenny moves straight to completing it (booking the slot, adding to cart, confirming the order).

* If they're not sure yet, Zenny asks a helpful question or two to guide them — never a wall of options, never a hard sell.

**Step 4 — Action gets completed.**

Zenny books it, orders it, reserves it, or registers it — right there in the conversation. No redirect to a separate form unless that's genuinely the better path.

**Step 5 — If something's left unfinished, Zenny follows up.**

A gentle, well-timed reminder — never spammy, never repeated too often — brings distracted customers back to finish what they started.

**Step 6 — If it's beyond Zenny's depth, a human takes over.**

Complaints, high-value decisions, anything sensitive — Zenny recognizes its limits and hands off smoothly, with full context so the customer never has to repeat themselves.

**One-line summary for a hero section:**

*"Ask, decide, done — Zenny handles the whole conversation, start to finish."*

## **5\. Detailed Feature Breakdown**

### **24/7 Availability**

**What it does:** Responds to customers instantly, any time of day, any day of the week.

**Why it matters:** Most missed sales/leads happen simply because nobody answered in time. A customer messaging at 11pm shouldn't have to wait until 9am.

**Example:** A customer messages a plumbing business at 2am about a leak. Zenny responds immediately, gathers the details, and either books an emergency slot or queues an urgent callback — instead of the customer moving on to a competitor.

### **Chat \+ Voice**

**What it does:** Zenny works as text-based chat and as a spoken conversation (web-based voice call or phone line).

**Why it matters:** Not every customer wants to type — especially for urgent or complex needs. Voice gives people the option that feels natural to them.

**Example:** A customer calls in about scheduling instead of messaging — Zenny handles the call the same way it would a chat, just spoken.

### **Knows the Business**

**What it does:** Zenny is trained on the business's actual information — products, services, prices, policies, hours, FAQs — so its answers are accurate, not generic.

**Why it matters:** Customers get real answers instantly instead of "let me check and get back to you."

**Example:** "Do you offer gluten-free options?" gets answered immediately and correctly, without needing a staff member.

### **Completes Real Actions**

**What it does:** Zenny doesn't just answer questions — it finishes the task. Books the appointment, places the order, reserves the table, signs someone up.

**Why it matters:** Removing the extra step (forms, callbacks, "someone will contact you") is what actually increases completed bookings/sales.

**Example:** "I'd like a haircut Thursday afternoon" → Zenny checks availability and confirms a specific time, right in the chat.

### **Remembers the Conversation**

**What it does:** Zenny keeps track of what's already been said, so customers don't repeat themselves.

**Why it matters:** Nothing frustrates customers more than re-explaining their issue to someone (or something) new.

**Example:** A customer mentions a dietary restriction early on — Zenny factors that into recommendations later in the same conversation, without being asked again.

### **Follows Up Automatically**

**What it does:** If someone starts an order/booking/inquiry and doesn't finish, Zenny sends a well-timed, natural follow-up.

**Why it matters:** People get distracted, not disinterested. A good reminder recovers business that would otherwise just be lost.

**Example:** A customer adds items to a cart and leaves without checking out — a little later, Zenny sends a friendly nudge, not a pushy "BUY NOW" blast.

### **Escalates When It Should**

**What it does:** Zenny recognizes when a conversation needs a real person — a complaint, a high-value decision, anything sensitive or ambiguous — and hands off cleanly.

**Why it matters:** Knowing its limits is what makes Zenny trustworthy. It's not trying to replace humans everywhere, just handle what it can reliably do well.

**Example:** A customer asks for a refund — Zenny doesn't attempt to resolve it itself, it routes the conversation straight to a human with full context already attached.

### **Multi-Channel**

**What it does:** Works across the business's website, social DMs (Instagram/Facebook/WhatsApp), SMS, email, and voice.

**Why it matters:** Customers shouldn't have to find the "right" channel — Zenny meets them wherever they already are.

**Example:** A customer messages on Instagram instead of the website — same quality of help, same booking capability.

### **Calendar-Aware**

**What it does:** Connects with the business's existing calendar/scheduling setup to book appointments directly, avoiding double-bookings.

**Why it matters:** Real-time accuracy means Zenny never offers a slot that isn't actually available.

**Example:** Zenny only offers open time slots and books directly into the calendar the business already uses.

### **Connects to Business Tools**

**What it does:** Zenny plugs into the systems a business already runs on — online store, booking software, CRM — so it always works with real, current data instead of guessing.

**Why it matters:** Recommendations and confirmations are only as good as the data behind them. A connected system means Zenny never promises something the business can't deliver.

**Example:** A shopper asks "do you have this dress in a size 8?" — Zenny checks the store's real inventory before answering, and if it's in stock, offers to add it to the cart right there. If it's sold out, Zenny suggests a similar in-stock item instead of leaving the customer stuck.

### **Dashboard & Reporting**

**What it does:** Gives the business owner a simple view of what Zenny has been doing — conversations handled, bookings made, leads recovered.

**Why it matters:** Full visibility builds trust in the system — nothing is a black box.

**Example:** A business owner checks their dashboard weekly and sees exactly how many customers were helped and how many bookings Zenny completed.

## **7\. Actual Use-Case Scenarios**

**Scenario 1 — The Midnight Emergency**

A pipe bursts at 1am. The homeowner searches "emergency plumber near me" and messages the first business that responds. Zenny replies within seconds, asks two quick questions, and either books an emergency slot or queues a callback with all the details ready for the technician. The business wins the job simply by being the one that answered.

**Scenario 2 — The Abandoned Cart**

A shopper adds a jacket to their cart, gets distracted, and closes the tab. An hour later, Zenny sends a short, friendly message: "Still thinking about the jacket? It's still in your cart if you'd like to grab it." No pressure, no countdown timer — just a helpful nudge. The shopper comes back and completes the purchase.

**Scenario 3 — The Unsure Browser**

Someone messages a skincare brand: "I have sensitive skin, what should I use?" Instead of dumping a product list, Zenny asks one clarifying question, then recommends two relevant products. The customer feels understood, not sold to.

**Scenario 4 — The Booking That Almost Didn't Happen**

A customer starts booking a haircut appointment but stops mid-conversation. The next day, Zenny follows up once: "Still want to grab that Thursday slot? It's still open." The customer finishes booking — a sale that would've quietly disappeared otherwise.

**Scenario 5 — The Complaint That Needed a Human**

A customer messages upset about a delayed order. Zenny recognizes this isn't something it should try to resolve alone, acknowledges the frustration, and immediately routes the conversation to a real staff member — with the full conversation history attached, so the customer doesn't have to explain it all again.

**Scenario 6 — The Out-of-Stock Save**

A customer messages a store asking for a specific pair of running shoes in their size. Zenny checks the connected store inventory and sees they're sold out — but instead of a dead-end "not available," it recommends a similar in-stock model from the same brand. The customer buys the alternative instead of leaving to check a competitor's site.

**Scenario 7 — The Returning Customer**

A regular client messages a salon to rebook. Zenny recognizes them, sees their past visit history, and suggests the same stylist and service as last time — the customer just confirms a time, without re-explaining preferences they've already shared before.

**Scenario 8 — The Weekend Question**

A customer messages a consulting agency on a Saturday asking about pricing for a service. Instead of waiting until Monday for a reply, Zenny answers immediately, asks a couple of qualifying questions, and — if it's a good fit — books a discovery call for early the following week. The lead doesn't go cold over the weekend.

**Scenario 9 — The Multi-Channel Switch**

A customer starts a conversation on Instagram, then later messages the same business's website chat. Zenny recognizes the same customer and continues the conversation with context intact, instead of starting from zero.

## **8\. FAQ Content**

**Is Zenny a chatbot?**

Not in the old sense. Zenny doesn't just answer scripted questions — it holds real conversations, completes actual bookings/orders, and knows when to bring in a human.

**Will customers know they're talking to AI?**

Yes — Zenny doesn't pretend to be human. Being upfront about that actually builds more trust, not less.

**What happens if Zenny can't help with something?**

It hands the conversation off to a real person, along with the full context, so the customer never has to start over.

**Does Zenny replace our staff?**

No — it handles the repetitive, time-sensitive first response so staff can focus on the conversations that truly need a human touch.

**Can Zenny actually book appointments/take orders, or does it just answer questions?**

It completes the action directly — booking, ordering, reserving — inside the conversation itself, connected to the business's real calendar/systems.

**Does Zenny work outside the website?**

Yes — it can be reached through social DMs, SMS, email, and voice as well.

**Is Zenny pushy or sales-heavy?**

No — it's built to guide and inform, not pressure. No fake urgency, no manufactured scarcity.

**How do we know what Zenny is doing?**

Through a simple dashboard showing conversations handled, bookings made, and recovered leads — full visibility, no black box.

**Does setup require our team to be technical?**

No — Zenny is trained on the business's existing information and connects to tools the business already uses.

**What kind of businesses is Zenny a good fit for?**

Any business that talks to customers regularly — service providers, retailers, restaurants, clinics, agencies, and community organizations alike.

**Can Zenny connect to our online store?**

Yes — Zenny can connect to a business's existing online store to check real inventory and give live product recommendations, rather than guessing what's available.

**Will Zenny recommend something that's actually out of stock?**

No — because it checks real, live inventory through the connected store, it won't confidently recommend something that isn't actually available. If an item is sold out, it can suggest a similar in-stock option instead.

**Does Zenny work with the booking/scheduling tool we already use?**

Yes — Zenny connects to the business's existing calendar or scheduling system so every booking is real-time and accurate, with no double-bookings.

**Can Zenny recognize returning customers?**

Yes — when connected to the business's customer records, Zenny recognizes returning customers and can reference past visits/orders to make the conversation feel continuous, not repetitive.

**Is our customer data safe with Zenny?**

Zenny only accesses the systems and data the business chooses to connect, and follows standard data-handling practices — this is a good area to loop in the product/technical team for specifics before publishing detailed claims.

**How long does it take to set up Zenny for a new business?**

Setup time varies by business size and how many systems need connecting — a simple setup can be quick, more complex integrations take longer. Check with the product team for current timelines before quoting a number publicly.

**Can Zenny handle more than one language?**

Check with the product team for current language support before publishing this claim — don't assume without confirming.

## **9\. How to Showcase Zenny (Personality, Tone & Storyboard Ideas)**

*This section is for the design team — how to think about Zenny when creating visuals, copy, or storyboards. These are suggestions and direction, not fixed rules.*

### **9.1 The Core Idea to Design Around**

Zenny should always feel like **a genuinely helpful presence**, not a sales tool wearing a friendly mask. Every visual, headline, or storyboard should pass one simple test:

*"Does this make Zenny look like it's trying to help the customer, or trying to sell to them?"*

If a concept leans toward the second, it's off-brand — even if it's technically accurate.

### **9.2 Personality — What Zenny Should Feel Like**

* **Calm and capable** — like the one employee who always knows the answer and never seems flustered, even at 2am.

* **Warm, not performative** — friendly because it's genuinely useful, not because it's using exclamation points and forced enthusiasm.

* **Quietly confident** — Zenny doesn't need to prove itself loudly. It just handles things well, every time.

* **Honest** — never pretends to be human, never hides that it's AI, never oversells what it can do.

* **Present, not intrusive** — shows up right when needed, doesn't hover, doesn't nag.

**A useful mental model:** Zenny is the calm, competent coworker who picks up the phone on the first ring — not the eager salesperson who greets you the second you walk in the door.

### **9.3 Personality — What Zenny Should NOT Feel Like**

* **Not a "salesy" bot.** No exclamation-heavy copy, no "LIMITED TIME," no countdown timers, no fake scarcity ("only 2 left\!"). If a line could appear in a spam text, rewrite it.

* **Not a gimmick/mascot.** Avoid cutesy cartoon characters, googly eyes, exaggerated "cute robot" designs — this undercuts trust for a product businesses rely on.

* **Not cold or robotic.** Avoid sterile, all-blue "tech" imagery, circuit-board patterns, or stiff corporate stock-photo energy.

* **Not overly humanlike.** Don't depict Zenny as a photorealistic human face or imply a human is secretly on the other end — it should read clearly as a capable AI, comfortable in its own identity.

* **Not desperate for attention.** No aggressive pop-ups, flashing elements, or anything that feels like it's begging for a click. Confidence doesn't need volume.

### **9.6 Quick Gut-Check Before Publishing Any Zenny Visual**

Ask:

1. Does this feel like genuine help, or like a sales push?

2. Would this make sense inside a real website/app, or does it look like an ad gimmick?

3. Is Zenny clearly AI, without pretending to be human or feeling cold/robotic?

4. Is there any fake urgency, scarcity, or pressure language in the copy?

5. Does it feel calm and confident, rather than loud and attention-grabbing?

If all five check out, it's on-brand.
